A clean table holds a white candle, 1 glass of water, and a single white rose. It faces an image of a skeletal figure in a white robe. This isn't a grand temple. It's a small public table for a quiet conversation. It's a Niña Blanca altar.

Her space is for cleansing and peace. A Niña Blanca altar becomes a seven-day container for clarity and home blessing, and you don't need expensive tools to start.



## What This Is

This is a guide to building a dedicated altar for Niña Blanca, the white aspect of Santa Muerte. She's a gentle face. Her work is purification: she washes away bad luck, and she creates a shield. This altar uses white and silver, then blue, to support that work. The practice includes a simple seven-day novena that brings peace and clarity to a home. The altar is sacred space, the place you offer prayers and light candles. It's a personal act of faith.

## What This Is Not

This isn't a crime-as-sacrament. It's not for hexes. This house doesn't sell harm. The white robe isn't for skull covers, and it isn't for threats. Niña Blanca's altar isn't shared. She requests her own dedicated space. You don't need an expensive statue. Devotion is about intention, not money.

## Choosing Niña Blanca's Sacred Space and Image



![Niña Blanca altar — section 1](/journal/nina-blanca-altar-guide-section-1.jpg)



You start with a surface. A shelf or a small table will work. In Tepito, Mexico City, you might see these in a quiet corner. Keep the space clean and dedicated only to her, a place you can tend to daily. You need an image. This can be a printed picture. A small statue works, too. Niña Blanca is a skeletal figure in a flowing white robe. She may hold a scythe. Place this image at the center back of your altar. This is your focal point.

## White, Silver, and Blue Offerings: Symbolism and Practical Setup



![Niña Blanca altar — section 2](/journal/nina-blanca-altar-guide-section-2.jpg)



The colors on this altar have meaning. White is for purity. Silver is for clarity and the moon's light. Blue is for calm. Together they create an environment for renewal. Your offerings can be simple, and you can find them at a dollar store. White offerings include candles, roses, clean white cloth, or salt. Silver offerings might be a small mirror or a silver-colored coin. Blue offerings can be a blue glass for water or a small blue stone. Fresh water is one of the most important gifts because it shows care. Put 1 glass of water on the altar every day, and change it often.

## Arranging the Altar Step by Step



![Niña Blanca altar — section 3](/journal/nina-blanca-altar-guide-section-3.jpg)



Clean your chosen surface. Wipe it with water, then place your image of Niña Blanca at the center back. Lay down a white cloth. That cloth defines the sacred space. Place 1 glass of fresh water in front of her image, and add 1 white candle in a simple votive holder. Arrange your other offerings around these central items: white flowers in a small vase, a silver coin, a blue stone. You may include other traditional gifts like a piece of bread. These are acts of hospitality. Your altar doesn't need to be crowded. Start with a few items. It can grow over time. The key is that it feels respectful to you.

## Seven-Day Novena for Peace, Clarity, and Home Blessing



![Niña Blanca altar — section 4](/journal/nina-blanca-altar-guide-section-4.jpg)



A novena is 9 days of prayer. This one is condensed to seven days, a focused container of work. You'll light one candle each day and offer a simple prayer. On day one, light your white candle, sit before your altar, and say aloud: "Niña Blanca, I invite your gentle presence. Please bless this home with peace." Let the candle burn safely for a while, then blow it out. On day two, light the candle again, offer fresh water, and say: "Niña Blanca, wash away any confusion. Bring clarity to my mind and heart." Repeat this for five more days. Each day, state your request. You can also sit silently and watch the candle flame. That's a form of meditation. On the seventh day, let your candle burn down completely if it's safe. Thank Niña Blanca for her attention. You can say: "Thank you for your protective shield." After the novena, keep tending the altar daily. Offer fresh water. Light candles when you wish to pray.

## Limits

A Niña Blanca altar is for spiritual support. It isn't a replacement for a doctor. If you're ill, see a doctor. If you've legal trouble, consult a lawyer. Keep your candle on a stable surface. Never leave a burning candle unattended. Use common fire safety. This altar is a personal devotion. Don't use it to attempt to control others. Petitions should be for your own peace. Her grace is for cleansing, not for forcing someone's will.

## Questions People Actually Ask

### Can I use a different colored candle if I don't have a white one?

Yes. Your intention matters most. A clear or light blue candle can work. The traditional color is white, but she understands sincerity.

### What if I need to travel or put the altar away?

You can respectfully wrap your image and items in a clean cloth. Put them in a safe place. You can say a brief goodbye. When you return, set it up again. She understands life.

### Do I have to pray in Spanish or use specific Catholic prayers?

No. You can pray in your own words. Speak from your heart. Many devotees pray in English or Spanish. The connection is in your honest intention.

### How often should I change the water?

Change the water daily if you can. It's a sign of fresh care. If you can't, change it every two or three days. Changing it is the practice.

### Can I add photos of my family to the altar for protection?

It's best to keep a Niña Blanca altar dedicated only to her. For family protection, you can place a separate small shelf nearby. Keep the main altar space focused on your relationship with her.

Tend your altar. Offer clean water. Light a candle when you need a moment of peace. This small public table is a witness to your desire for a clean start.
